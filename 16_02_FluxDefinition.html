Understanding Flux

Flux \(J\) is defined as the amount of a property crossing a unit area per unit time. Pipe flow provides a visual way to understand this density.

1. Volume Flow Rate \(Q\)

In steady state flow, the total volume passing through the system per unit time is constant.

$$ Q = \frac{dV}{dt} = \text{Constant} $$

2. Area and Velocity

Since \(Q = A \cdot v\), if the area \(A\) decreases, the velocity \(v\) must increase to maintain flow.

Large Area \(A\) Low Velocity \(v\)
Small Area \(A\) High Velocity \(v\)

3. Flux Intensity \(J\)

Flux is the normalization of flow rate by the surface area it crosses.

$$ J = \frac{\text{Flow Rate }(Q)}{\text{Area }(A)} $$

Insight: For a fixed flow rate, a smaller area concentrates the transport, resulting in a higher flux.
